kirin Privacy Policy

Effective October 14, 2026

kirin is an iOS keyboard that checks the English you write. This policy explains what kirin sends, what is kept, and for how long. kirin is made by Taishi Kato, an individual developer (“we”).

In short

  • kirin sends your text only when you tap “Check All” or “Check meaning in Japanese” on the keyboard.
  • Our server does not store your text.
  • OpenAI checks the text. It does not use it to train its models, and keeps logs for up to 30 days to detect abuse.
  • Google translates the text into Japanese when you ask. It does not use it to improve its products, and keeps logs for 55 days to detect abuse.
  • kirin has no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking.

What kirin sends, and when

  • kirin sends text only when you tap “Check All” or “Check meaning in Japanese” on the kirin keyboard. Showing the keyboard or typing with its keys sends nothing.
  • For “Check All”, it sends the full text of the current input field, and the language of the app’s screen so explanations come back in that language. This happens in any app where you use the keyboard, not only X.
  • “Check meaning in Japanese” sends only the full text of the current input field.
  • Text that contains no Latin letters is not sent. If any part has Latin letters, such as a URL, the whole text is sent.
  • The text goes through our own server, which runs on Cloudflare Workers, to a language model. For “Check All”, it goes to OpenAI’s API, which checks it and suggests fixes and alternative phrasings. For “Check meaning in Japanese”, it goes to Google’s Gemini API, which translates it into Japanese the way a native English reader would understand it.

What our server keeps

  • Your text: not stored. The server passes it to OpenAI or Google and returns the result.
  • Device registration: to stop abuse, each installation of the app registers with Apple’s App Attest. The server keeps a random key identifier created on your device, a public key, a counter, a one-way hash of the device token, and how many requests the installation made in the current minute and day. This record contains no name, email address, phone number, or text. It is kept while the service runs.
  • IP address: when the app registers a device, the server keeps a keyed one-way hash of your IP address for up to one hour, to limit how often registration can be tried. The IP address itself is not stored.
  • Logs: each request leaves a short log line with the result, error codes, the length of the text, and how long it took. Logs never contain your text, your device token, or your IP address.

Service providers

  • OpenAI receives the text you check. We call its API with storage turned off. OpenAI does not use text sent through its API to train its models. To detect abuse, it keeps logs that may include the text for up to 30 days, and longer only when the law requires it or to prevent harm. See OpenAI’s privacy policy.
  • Google receives the text you ask to translate, through the paid tier of the Gemini API. Google does not use text sent on the paid tier to improve its products. It keeps logs that include the text for 55 days to detect abuse and for legally required disclosures. See the Gemini API terms and Google’s privacy policy.
  • Cloudflare runs our server. Like any network service, it handles your IP address and request data to deliver requests. See Cloudflare’s privacy policy.
  • Apple provides App Attest, which the app uses to prove it is a genuine copy. Your device talks to Apple for this; our server does not.

We do not sell or share your data with anyone else, and we do not use it for advertising.

What stays on your device

  • The device token and registration data, in the iOS Keychain. They are not synced to iCloud.
  • When you replace your text with a suggestion, the original text is saved in the Keychain first, in case the keyboard closes mid-replacement. It is deleted as soon as the replacement finishes. If the keyboard closes midway, it is kept until you choose to restore or discard it the next time you open the keyboard or the app.
  • Your settings, such as consent and the screen language, in storage shared by the app and the keyboard.

Full Access

iOS requires “Allow Full Access” for a keyboard to use the network. kirin uses it only to send your text when you tap “Check All” or “Check meaning in Japanese”. Without Full Access, you can still type with kirin, and nothing is sent.

Your choices

  • Nothing is sent until you agree on the app’s consent screen.
  • You can stop sending at any time. Turn off the kirin keyboard or its Full Access in iOS Settings, or delete the app.
  • Because the server keeps no name, email address, or text, we cannot match a server record to a person. If you have a question or a request about your data, contact us below.

Children

kirin is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

We will update this page when this policy changes. If what kirin sends or who receives it changes, the app will ask for your consent again before sending anything.
